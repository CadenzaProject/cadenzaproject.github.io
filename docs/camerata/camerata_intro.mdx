The Cadenza project is researching how to improve music for people with hearing loss.

On Thursday 29 November 2026, together with Manchester Camerata, we held a free concert at Manchester's Stoller Hall.
It was a unique opportunity for the audience to help shape future research on hearing aids while enjoying live music.

The concert featured Beethoven's Symphony No. 6, the "Pastoral". Over 200 people attended and provided valuable data for the project.

The Camerata opened by playing a movement from the symphony.
The orchestra then performed short excerpts in different ways to explore common problems faced by people with hearing loss.
For example, loud sounds can become uncomfortable, so in one excerpt the Camerata held back at the peak of a big crescendo to see whether that helped.
The audience rated each version using a simple questionnaire.

The event ended with a short Q&A, where audience members asked experts from the Cadenza project about hearing loss, hearing aids and listening to music.


### Cadenza

The Cadenza Project is a 5-year UKRI-funded research project involving the Universities of Salford, Sheffield, Nottingham and Leeds with associated partners RNID, Logitech, University of Oldenburg, Sonova and BBC.
We are researching how hearing loss harms listening to music and working to improve it.
Ethical approval for the research concert will come from the University of Leeds.
